PROBLEM STATEMENT Organizations often struggle to identify employees who may be experiencing excessive workload pressure, reduced motivation, or difficulties adapting to new responsibilities. These concerns are frequently identified only after they begin affecting employee performance, engagement, or overall workplace wellbeing. Managers may have access to task assignments and project progress, but they do not necessarily have sufficient insight into employees' experiences, motivation, learning confidence, and perceived workplace support. Our proposed project aims to address this gap through an Organizational Behavior-based system that assesses employee experiences, identifies potential support needs, and recommends appropriate managerial interventions.

----- PAGE 4 -----

PROPOSED SOLUTION We propose developing a web-based Employee Behavioral Insights & Adaptive Support System. The system will collect two types of information: Work-related information, such as assigned tasks, estimated effort, deadlines, and task progress. Employee-reported information through periodic check-ins covering workload pressure, motivation, managerial support, autonomy, and learning confidence.
Using established Organizational Behavior theories and a defined assessment methodology, the system will generate separate employee indicators, identify potential behavioral and workplace support needs, and recommend suitable managerial actions. The system will also maintain historical records to help managers monitor changes following interventions.
OB CONCEPTS BEING USED
The project will primarily draw upon three established theories:
1. Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory: To understand employee satisfaction, dissatisfaction, and motivation (workplace satisfaction factors). 2. Self-Determination Theory (SDT): To assess employee motivation through autonomy, competence, and relatedness (psychological needs underlying motivation). 3. Social Cognitive Theory: To understand the role of self-efficacy and confidence in employee learning and task performance.
These theories will guide the selection of assessment dimensions, questionnaire design, classification methodology, and managerial recommendations.

----- PAGE 5 -----

PROPOSED APPLICATION FEATURES
• Employee profiles and organizational structure. • Workload and task information management. • Periodic employee pulse check-ins. • Separate workload, motivation, and learning-confidence indicators. • Rule-based employee assessment and classification. • Personalized managerial support recommendations. • Employee and manager dashboards. • Historical trends and intervention tracking.
RESEARCH COMPONENT
The development process will include: • A literature review of the selected OB theories and relevant academic research. • A survey of working individuals to understand workload, motivation, and workplace support. • Interviews with managers or HR professionals to understand current employee-support practices. • Research-based questionnaire and assessment design. • Pilot testing and evaluation of the developed application.

----- PAGE 6 -----

EXPECTED OUTCOME The expected outcome is a functional web application that demonstrates how Organizational Behavior theories can be translated into a practical managerial support tool. The project will go beyond basic employee record management by incorporating research-based assessment, behavioral classification, recommendation logic, and historical analysis. Our objective is to develop a prototype that demonstrates how organizations can identify potential employee support needs earlier and make more informed, employee-centered managerial decisions.
